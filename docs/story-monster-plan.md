# Story and monster plan

Status: design proposal for approval. This document does not change combat, progression, saves, or shipped artwork.

## Direction summary

- Story: **The Shattered Skyheart**.
- Present release: Intro, Battle 10 story page, and Battle 20 story page with a Stage 3 cliffhanger.
- Future release: Battle 30 story page and a separate ending page.
- Each page uses three silent images. Artwork contains no captions, dialogue, names, or level text.
- Combat roster through Battle 20 uses **11 combat illustrations**: three reusable normal monsters, six reusable elites, and two unique bosses.
- The complete present-release art scope is **20 shipping illustrations**: 11 combat images plus nine panels across the Intro, Battle 10, and Battle 20 pages. Reference sheets are additional working files, not runtime art.
- Normal monsters and reusable elites show `Lv. #` beside their names. Only Battles 10 and 20 use the **Boss** label.
- Stage 3, Crownwing, and the true ending are story-bible material only while Stage 3 remains Coming soon.

## Audience and tone

The working audience is broad casual and family-friendly fantasy players who respond to cute creatures, visible progression, and dramatic bosses. This is not a child-only positioning decision; a more specific marketing demographic can be chosen later.

The tone should be:

- bright, adventurous, and sincere;
- readable on a small landscape phone;
- exciting without gore or horror;
- emotionally simple: danger, courage, discovery, mercy, restoration;
- cute for normal monsters, impressive for elites, and iconic for bosses.

Names should be one or two words, easy to pronounce, visually descriptive, and distinct when scanned quickly. The hero remains unnamed in the story because the player can rename the adventurer.

Story pages always use one canonical hero: the original Starter outfit, Wayfarer blade, no shield, and teal scarf. This fixed comic appearance is independent of the player's chosen name, scarf color, and equipped set. The teal scarf is the hero's recurring visual identifier.

A mascot companion can be explored later, but this story does not depend on one before a companion exists in gameplay.

## Story canon

SkyHaven and the surrounding Cloudring islands are held together by the **Skyheart**, a warm golden crystal protected in the town's central shrine. A dark relic called the **Hollow Crown** twists the great guardian Crownwing. Crownwing strikes the Skyheart and scatters its three main shards across the islands.

The fallen shards frighten ordinary wildlife and overcharge the strongest guardians. The adventurer follows the shard trails through Cloudring, breaks the dark growth around each guardian, and recovers the light without killing the creature. Brambleback holds the green shard, Skybreaker holds the blue shard, and Crownwing keeps the final gold shard at Nightspire.

The Hollow Crown is a one-off story object, not a player relic or a new gameplay system. Normal monsters do not become evil species; they are displaced or agitated creatures. This lets a victory end in cleansing and recovery rather than death.

The three shards must remain recognizable without color:

- green shard: a rounded leaf silhouette with a forked tip;
- blue shard: a four-point diamond with one lightning-shaped notch;
- gold shard: a long feather silhouette that Crownwing catches and retains during the shattering.

The Hollow Crown is black metal with sharp inward points. It must not resemble Brambleback's organic thorn crown or Skybreaker's broken stone halo.

## Silent story pages

Each page follows the same visual grammar as the supplied comic reference without borrowing its dark style or plot:

- Panel 1, upper left: setup or threat.
- Panel 2, lower left: the decisive action or discovery.
- Panel 3, full height on the right: the consequence and next destination.
- One clear action per panel.
- Repeat the same important object across panels so the viewer can follow cause and effect.
- Use gaze, motion, light trails, and silhouette instead of explanatory symbols or text.
- Aim decisive strikes at the visible binding, halo, or Hollow Crown rather than at a creature's body.
- Communicate cleansing through posture as well as color: softened eyes, lowered horns or wings, a sheathed sword, and a guardian deliberately revealing the next path.
- Keep faces, the scarf, each shard, and the next destination inside crop-safe central areas.

Generate the three panels as separate images and assemble them in the game. This gives better character consistency, responsive cropping, and replacement control than asking an image model to draw an entire comic page with borders.

### Page 1 — Intro

Trigger: once for a new adventure, before the first arrival in SkyHaven. It may be skipped. A replay archive is optional later scope rather than a requirement for the first art integration.

1. SkyHaven is peaceful beneath the glowing Skyheart. Crownwing circles as its calm guardian while the Hollow Crown descends behind it; the small teal-scarf adventurer trains in the foreground.
2. The Crown closes around Crownwing and twists its posture. Bound Crownwing strikes the Skyheart: the leaf-shaped green and notched blue shards streak away while Crownwing catches the feather-shaped gold shard in one talon.
3. The island paths dim and begin to separate. A green trail points into Cloudring while the hero grips the sword and the teal scarf streams in the same direction.

### Page 2 — Battle 10 clear

Trigger: after the first reward settlement for Battle 10 and before Stage 2 selection.

1. Brambleback blocks the path, a green Skyheart shard trapped within its crown of violet thorns.
2. The hero's clean sword strike breaks the dark thorn binding. The shard rises intact while the guardian's angry glow fades.
3. The now-calm Brambleback lowers its head while the hero sheathes the sword. The guardian deliberately turns toward a wind path cast by the green shard, leading to a distant island covered by blue storm clouds.

### Page 3 — Battle 20 clear and Stage 3 cliffhanger

Trigger: after the first reward settlement for Battle 20. This is the present release's final story page.

1. Skybreaker coils around the blue shard, its cloud-and-stone body held together by cyan lightning and a broken halo.
2. The hero strikes the broken halo rather than the creature. The binding breaks, the storm parts, and Skybreaker lowers its head and loosens its coils as the blue shard rises.
3. The leaf-shaped green and notched blue shards hover together and project one path toward Nightspire. At its end is one simple silhouette: Crownwing beneath the Hollow Crown, visibly holding the feather-shaped gold shard. The ordinary interface may show **Stage 3 Coming soon** after the page; the artwork itself remains text-free.

### Page 4 — Battle 30 clear, future

Trigger: after the future first reward settlement for Battle 30.

1. The hero reaches Nightspire while Crownwing guards the final gold shard beneath the Hollow Crown.
2. The decisive strike cracks the crown rather than Crownwing. Darkness tears away from the guardian in a clear outward motion.
3. The cleansed Crownwing offers the feather-shaped gold shard while the green and blue shards rise beside it. The three recovered shapes fill the panel; Skyheart reformation waits for the Ending page.

### Page 5 — Ending, future

Trigger: immediately after the future Battle 30 page, with a separate Skip action so the climax and epilogue remain distinct.

1. The hero places the green, blue, and gold shards into the empty shrine.
2. The Skyheart reforms. Broad rings of golden wind reconnect the islands and relight the paths.
3. Return to the Intro's shrine composition as a visual bookend. The hero and three cleansed guardians stand prominently beneath the restored Skyheart; normal creatures appear only as peaceful background silhouettes. The teal scarf lifts in a calm wind.

## Monster roles

Every encounter keeps its existing three-wave structure:

1. reusable normal monster;
2. reusable normal monster;
3. elite, except the third wave of Battles 10 and 20 is a stage boss.

The current game calls every third enemy a Boss. The future art integration should change ordinary third enemies to **Elite** and reserve **Boss** for Battles 10 and 20. This is a presentation correction, not a balance change.

### Unique bosses

#### Battle 10 — Brambleback

- Role: ancient Bloomwild guardian carrying the green shard.
- Silhouette: giant stag-treant with a heavy quadruped body, crown-like antlers, and a broad leafy back.
- Palette: forest green, warm bark, cream antlers, violet corruption, green-gold shard.
- Recognition feature: the shard is visible within the thorn crown, making the story objective readable immediately.
- Emotional range: intimidating while bound, gentle when cleansed.
- Combat asset scope: bound state only. Its cleansed appearance belongs to reference art and story panels, not a second combat sprite.

#### Battle 20 — Skybreaker

- Role: Stormpeak guardian carrying the blue shard and the Stage 2/current content-cap boss.
- Silhouette: a coiled cloud dragon built from storm cloud, floating-island stone, and one broken halo.
- Palette: deep blue, slate, white cloud edges, electric cyan, blue-gold shard.
- Recognition feature: its curved body frames the bright core, producing a different silhouette from every ground monster.
- Emotional range: violent storm while bound, protective guardian when cleansed.
- Combat asset scope: bound state only. Its cleansed appearance belongs to reference art and story panels, not a second combat sprite.

#### Battle 30 — Crownwing, future

- Role: original Skyheart guardian controlled by the Hollow Crown.
- Silhouette: a large bird-dragon with a crown-shaped wing spread.
- Palette: midnight blue and violet under corruption, returning to white, teal, and gold when cleansed.
- Recognition feature: the black crown and gold shard remain visible in every story appearance.
- Do not create the combat asset while Stage 3 is Coming soon unless it is needed as a distant silhouette in the Battle 20 story page.

## Combat image specification

Use the first supplied image only for its bright sky-island mood, friendly proportions, sunlight, painterly finish, and clear silhouettes. Do not copy its exact characters or scenery. Use the second supplied image only for three-panel visual storytelling and cause-and-effect composition, not for its dark palette, tower, powers, or ending.

Every monster combat image should have:

- a 1024 × 1024 RGBA master with one original creature on a transparent background;
- no scenery, text, interface, frame, cast shadow, attack effect, or baked level marker;
- full body in three-quarter view, facing left toward the hero;
- the same floor line at approximately 89% of image height, scale convention, light direction, and padding;
- at least 8% transparent clearance from every edge, centered body mass, and clean alpha without a white fringe;
- a silhouette readable at approximately 120–160 CSS pixels;
- no cropped horns, wings, tail, feet, or floating pieces;
- no more than four dominant colors, plus small glow accents;
- warm light from the upper left;
- enough empty space for the existing hit flash, defeat rotation, and HP/name area;
- one calm idle pose. Additional attack or defeat poses should wait until the base roster is approved.

Relative on-screen scale:

| Role | Approximate height versus hero | Shape language |
|---|---:|---|
| Normal | 65–80% | Rounded and compact |
| Elite | 95–115% | One enlarged defining feature |
| Boss | 135–160% | Unique silhouette with a readable core |

## Production order

1. Approve this story, the three stage themes, and all names.
2. Generate Mossling, Flutterbat, Pebbleguard, Glowmoth, Brambleback, and Skybreaker separately. Then assemble those approved images with a canonical hero scale silhouette into a reference lineup. Do not ask an image model to create the full lineup in one image, because identities and scale may blend.
3. Generate and approve the three normal combat assets.
4. Generate the three Stage 1 elites and Brambleback.
5. Generate the three Stage 2 elites and Skybreaker.
6. Check one focused 568 × 320 combat preview with the hero, one normal, Glowmoth, and Skybreaker before integrating the 11 approved combat images. Keep battle numbers, stats, rewards, and save progression unchanged.
7. Create the nine current-release story panels only after the hero, Brambleback, Skybreaker, Skyheart, shards, and Hollow Crown have approved reference designs.
8. Defer the six Battle 30 and ending panels, the Crownwing combat asset, and all Stage 3 enemies until Stage 3 production begins.

## Approval gates

Before producing the whole roster, approve:

- story premise and ending;
- Brambleback, Skybreaker, and Crownwing names;
- whether the audience tone is cute-adventurous enough or should be older/darker;
- the assembled reference lineup with the canonical hero scale silhouette;
- one normal monster at actual phone size;
- one elite and one boss beside the hero at actual combat scale;
- the Intro page as the first silent-story proof before producing the other pages.

## Later integration boundaries

- Keep 20 active battles, three waves per battle, current combat tuning, rewards, unlocks, stable equipment IDs, and old saves.
- Store encounter role, name, and art ID with gameplay descriptors in `core.js`; derive the visible monster level from the battle number so it cannot drift out of sync.
- Store replaceable image paths, crops, pivots, and scale descriptors in `art.js`.
- When story art ships, Battles 10 and 20 need a boss-specific cleansing transition instead of the generic collapse-and-fade ending: the dark binding fades while the guardian remains visibly calm until the page transition.
- Use the sequence: victory animation and once-only reward settlement → compact result → first-clear story page → Arena. The story page never controls or repeats the reward.
- Mark a milestone story as seen only when Continue or Skip is chosen. If the app closes mid-page, the already-settled reward remains safe and the page can appear again. Existing saves that already cleared a milestone should not be interrupted automatically during migration.
- A future replay archive may expose seen pages without changing rewards or progression, but it is separate optional UI scope.
- Preserve visibility and orientation pausing. A story page must never reload or interrupt an active training or arena run.
- Add all approved runtime images to `dist/sw.js` and bump the cache name in the release that ships them.
- Do not publish until Jimmy explicitly requests publication.
